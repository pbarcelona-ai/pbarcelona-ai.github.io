UART fractional baud generator: any baud rate from any clock

Published 2026-10-01

These notes cover the baud-rate generator inside the UART core: a 32-bit phase accumulator instead of the usual integer divider. It lets the UART hit standard baud rates almost exactly from whatever clock the rest of the design runs on, with no PLL and no special crystal.

The problem with integer dividers

A classic UART divides the system clock by an integer to get a 16× oversampling tick. That only works when the clock divides evenly. From a 100 MHz clock:

BaudIdeal divisor (fclk / 16·baud)Integer divider errorFractional generator error
115,20054.25+0.47%−0.003 ppm
921,6006.78−3.1%+0.001 ppm
1,000,0006.25+4.2% (÷6) or −10.7% (÷7)−0.001 ppm

A UART link typically tolerates around 2–3% combined error between the two ends before sampling drifts into the wrong bit by the stop bit. An integer divider at 921,600 baud from 100 MHz is already outside that.

Phase accumulator: carry out is the tick

wire [PHASE_W:0] sum = {1'b0, acc} + {1'b0, fcw};
always_ff @(posedge clk) begin
  acc      <= sum[PHASE_W-1:0];
  tick16_o <= sum[PHASE_W];          // carry = 16x oversampling tick
end

Each clock adds the frequency control word fcw to a 32-bit accumulator. The carry out of the top bit fires on average fcw / 232 times per clock, so:

FCW = round(baud × 16 × 232 / f_clk)

The resolution is f_clk / (16 × 232), about 0.0015 baud at 100 MHz. The average rate is effectively exact. Individual ticks are spaced either ⌊N⌋ or ⌈N⌉ clocks apart (54 or 55 clocks at 115,200 baud), so each tick carries up to one clock of jitter. That's about 0.1% of a bit at 115,200 baud (868 clocks per bit) and still only 1% at 1 Mbaud, and it doesn't accumulate.

Worked FCW values

ClockBaudFCW
100 MHz115,20032'h04B7F5A5 (79,164,837)
100 MHz921,60032'h25BFAD2A (633,318,698)
100 MHz1,000,00032'h28F5C28F (687,194,767)
50 MHz115,20032'h096FEB4A (158,329,674)

You rarely compute these by hand. uart_top derives the reset value of FCW at elaboration from its CLK_HZ and BAUD parameters, rounding to nearest, and software can change baud rate at run time by writing register 0x04. The one hard limit: FCW must fit in 32 bits, so the baud rate must stay below f_clk / 16 (6.25 Mbaud at 100 MHz).

The carry is registered before it leaves the generator, so the tick costs one flop and a 32-bit adder, with no long combinational path into the transmitter and receiver.

The receiver: sampling mid-bit

uart_rx runs from the same 16× tick:

  • rxd passes through a two-flop synchronizer (async_reg), because the serial line is asynchronous to the FPGA clock.
  • The receiver only arms after it has seen the line idle (high), so a held-low line (a break) doesn't produce a stream of garbage frames.
  • On a falling edge it counts 8 ticks to the middle of the start bit and checks the line is still low. If not, the edge was a glitch and is ignored.
  • From there it samples every 16 ticks, in the middle of each data, parity and stop bit. 5 to 8 data bits and odd or even parity are supported.
  • A low stop bit raises frame_err; a parity mismatch raises parity_err. Both are reported with the received word.

Sampling mid-bit is what tolerates baud mismatch: the sample point can drift almost half a bit over a frame before it lands in the wrong bit. The fractional generator leaves essentially all of that margin for the other end of the link.

Data moves over AXI4-Stream: bytes to transmit go in on s_axis, received bytes come out on m_axis. Transmit and receive FIFOs (FIFO_DEPTH, default 512, in block RAM) sit between the streams and the serial logic.

Verification and size

The self-checking testbench runs at 1 Mbaud from a 100 MHz clock, a rate an integer divider can't produce accurately. It checks transmit framing and parity with a serial monitor, receive through a serial driver, internal loopback, the error flags with write-1-to-clear, and register readback. Yosys (synth_xilinx, 7-series) estimates the full UART with its AXI4-Lite registers and both FIFOs at about 627 LUTs, 830 flip-flops and 3 BRAM18.
